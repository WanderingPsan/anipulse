# Findings: what is associated with a higher MyAnimeList score?

This file is generated by `python -m analysis.run` from the database. Every number in it is
filled in by code. Data last updated: 2026-10-09T08:38:54+00:00.

## Headline

Using only facts known before an anime airs, a linear model explains about 43% of the variation in MyAnimeList score on titles it has not seen (5-fold cross-validated R squared 0.43, versus 0.00 for always predicting the mean). No single factor has a large effect on its own: the biggest is medium. The strongest are studio (effect size 0.106, medium), source material (0.104, medium), and content rating (0.075, medium). These are associations in observational data, not causes.

## Read effect sizes first

The analysis population has 12,668 titles. With that many titles, almost any
difference is statistically significant, so **effect size is what matters**, not the p-value.
Here, 33 of the 60 tests are significant after correction (adjusted
p < 0.05), and 15 of those significant results have a negligible
effect size.

How to read the numbers:

- **Effect size** is the share of the variation in score ranks that a factor explains, from 0
  (nothing) to 1 (everything). It is rho squared for number factors (Spearman) and epsilon
  squared for category factors (Kruskal-Wallis), so the two can be compared.
- **Labels** use common rule-of-thumb cutoffs: below 0.01 is negligible, 0.01 to 0.06 small,
  0.06 to 0.14 medium, 0.14 and above large.
- **Adjusted p** is the p-value after a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all 60
  Spearman and Kruskal-Wallis tests in this report. Running many tests makes some come out
  "significant" by luck; the adjustment accounts for that. Raw p-values are shown next to it.

## Limitations

- **Association, not causation.** This is observational data. A studio's high median score
  may reflect the projects it is offered, not what it does to them.
- **MyAnimeList users are not everyone.** Scores reflect the people who use the site and
  choose to rate a title.
- **Only scored titles are included.** 9,935 titles in the database have no score
  and are left out.
- **The members filter favors better-known titles.** Requiring at least 1,000 members
  removed 4,452 scored titles, so obscure titles are under-represented. Within the
  population, the least popular tenth has a median score of 5.96 and the
  most popular tenth 7.67, so the titles left out likely score lower still.
- **Range restriction.** Within a narrow band of scores, such as the top 250, effects shrink.
  See Question 3.
- **Recent years are incomplete.** The snapshot is from July 2025. Titles dated after 2024
  (745 in the catalog) are partly from the snapshot and partly from the live API's
  top-anime pages, so they are not a full picture of those years.
- **Many tests.** Adjusted p-values control for running 60 tests together, but effect
  sizes are still the safer guide.
