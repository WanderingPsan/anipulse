## Read effect sizes first

The analysis population has $population_n titles. With that many titles, almost any
difference is statistically significant, so **effect size is what matters**, not the p-value.
Here, $n_significant of the $n_tests tests are significant after correction (adjusted
p < 0.05), and $n_significant_negligible of those significant results have a negligible
effect size.

How to read the numbers:

- **Effect size** is the share of the variation in score ranks that a factor explains, from 0
  (nothing) to 1 (everything). It is rho squared for number factors (Spearman) and epsilon
  squared for category factors (Kruskal-Wallis), so the two can be compared.
- **Labels** use common rule-of-thumb cutoffs: below 0.01 is negligible, 0.01 to 0.06 small,
  0.06 to 0.14 medium, 0.14 and above large.
- **Adjusted p** is the p-value after a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all $n_tests
  Spearman and Kruskal-Wallis tests in this report. Running many tests makes some come out
  "significant" by luck; the adjustment accounts for that. Raw p-values are shown next to it.

The top 250 is also a much smaller sample, so its tests have less power to detect anything.
The fairer comparison is the gaps and effect sizes, not the count of significant tests. The
pattern is consistent with range restriction: inside the top 250 every score is high, so there
is little room for any factor to separate titles. A factor that matters across the whole
catalog can look irrelevant among the best shows.

## Limitations

- **Association, not causation.** This is observational data. A studio's high median score
  may reflect the projects it is offered, not what it does to them.
- **MyAnimeList users are not everyone.** Scores reflect the people who use the site and
  choose to rate a title.
- **Only scored titles are included.** $no_score_removed titles in the database have no score
  and are left out.
- **The members filter favors better-known titles.** Requiring at least 1,000 members
  removed $members_removed scored titles, so obscure titles are under-represented. Within the
  population, the least popular tenth has a median score of $lowest_decile_median and the
  most popular tenth $highest_decile_median, so the titles left out likely score lower still.
- **Range restriction.** Within a narrow band of scores, such as the top 250, effects shrink.
  See Question 3.
- **Recent years are incomplete.** The snapshot is from July 2025. Titles dated after $last_full_year
  ($recent_titles in the catalog) are partly from the snapshot and partly from the live API's
  top-anime pages, so they are not a full picture of those years.
- **Many tests.** Adjusted p-values control for running $n_tests tests together, but effect
  sizes are still the safer guide.
